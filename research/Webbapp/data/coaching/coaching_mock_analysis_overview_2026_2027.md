## 2027 question architecture

### VisionIAS 2027 Test 15601
The supplied paper is a 100-question GS-I style booklet with 2 hours/200 marks and negative marking. The paper is strongly concentrated on Indian Constitution/Polity because it is a sectional/fundamental-style test.

Observed question architecture from the 100-question paper:
- Multi-statement: 48
- Direct/single-answer: 46
- Count/how-many: 4
- Scenario/case-like: 1
- Sequence/order: 1

The paper repeatedly uses constitutional provisions, institutional functions, rights, amendments, Parliament, courts, constitutional bodies and governance. It also inserts contemporary/contextual references into otherwise static polity questions.

Important lesson: a sectional paper should not be judged by the full UPSC subject distribution, but it must train the same reasoning grammar. This Vision paper does expose statement-based reasoning, but the final engine should ensure the question pool does not become repetitive around one template.

## 2027 cross-provider learning

### What to learn
1. **Sectional tests need internal variety.**
   Do not let a subject test become 100 versions of the same statement pattern.

2. **Concept + application should coexist.**
   Forum's sample contains conceptual questions, institutional questions and situation-based questions. The final database should store these as separate format/skill tags.

3. **Static and contemporary linkage must be explicit.**
   A contemporary reference should lead to a static concept, institution, law, mechanism, geography, technology or implication—not merely test recognition of the news item.

4. **A sectional paper is not a mini full-length.**
   Its subject weight should follow the test blueprint, but its question grammar should still train UPSC-like elimination.

5. **Question IDs must be permanent.**
   The same underlying question/concept should not re-enter the active mock pool merely through wording changes.

### The major lesson from the 2026 sample
The two supplied Vision 2026 papers have 51 and 61 direct questions respectively. That is materially more direct-answer-heavy than the official 2026 paper's 19-question direct bucket.

This does NOT prove that the entire coaching series was wrong, because only two distinct 2026 papers are available here. But it is a strong warning for our database:

> **Do not allow sectional/mock-generation logic to drift into a direct-fact-heavy distribution when the target full-length paper is statement/elimination/inference heavy.**

The final engine therefore needs separate controls for:
- subject coverage
- format architecture
- statement load
- reasoning/elimination burden
- current-affairs linkage
- source dependence
- difficulty
- cross-subject integration

## Specific mistakes we should avoid

### 1. Direct-fact inflation
A question being factually difficult does not make it UPSC-like.

Bad database behavior:
- obscure place + obscure fact
- obscure species + one-line identification
- obscure institution + direct function
- one current event + direct recall

Better:
- use the fact as one component of a multi-claim judgment
- connect it to a mechanism, constitutional provision, geography, technology, institution or implication
- create distractors around plausible misconceptions

### 2. Current affairs as a separate trivia layer
The 2026 UPSC pattern is better represented by current affairs being integrated with subject knowledge. The existing PYQ analysis records current affairs/IR as a major component while also noting that current-affairs cues are frequently embedded in subject questions. fileciteturn18file0

Database rule:
`Primary Subject` + `Topic` + `CA Linkage` must be separate fields.

### 3. Difficulty through obscurity
Do not label a question "hard" merely because the fact is obscure.

Difficulty should come from:
- number of claims
- closeness of distractors
- conceptual depth
- cross-topic integration
- elimination burden
- wording precision
- source convergence
- current/static linkage

### 4. Copying PYQ surface language
Never generate a question by changing names or nouns in a PYQ.

Use:
- same concept family
- different factual surface
- different proposition structure
- different distractor logic
- different application angle

The existing PYQ rules already require similarity/fingerprint checks for this reason. fileciteturn18file0

### 5. Treating coaching source lists as prediction evidence
Coaching sources are useful for mapping the preparation ecosystem, not for declaring what UPSC will ask.

The database must preserve:
`coaching_source` ≠ `UPSC_probability`.

### 6. Weak full-length calibration
A full-length paper cannot be produced by simply concatenating 10-15 sectional questions from each subject.

Full-length generation needs an independent blueprint:
- subject distribution
- format distribution
- difficulty distribution
- current/static distribution
- statement load
- elimination burden
- integrated questions
- question-order dynamics

### 7. No provenance
Every generated question should have:
- immutable Question ID
- source/provenance
- primary subject
- micro-topic
- static/current flag
- CA linkage
- format
- statement count
- elimination profile
- difficulty dimensions
- PYQ similarity fingerprint
- coaching similarity fingerprint
- verification status
- answer-key audit status

## 2027-specific learning from the two new papers

The 2027 papers are valuable mainly for **question-writing behavior**, not for predicting UPSC weightage.

Vision 2027 and Forum 2027 both demonstrate:
- strong use of multi-statement questions
- constitutional/institutional framing in Polity sectionals
- conceptual questions mixed with factual knowledge
- occasional scenario/application framing
- contemporary references inside static syllabus areas

The Forum paper also demonstrates longer contextual stems and several application-oriented questions. fileciteturn12file1L61-L127

## Final database rules locked after this review

### Generation pipeline
PYQ evidence
→ official 2026 grammar
→ long-run topic recurrence
→ recent trend shift
→ coaching coverage intelligence
→ coaching question-style learning
→ source/provenance verification
→ question generation
→ similarity scan
→ subject/format balance
→ answer verification
→ bilingual equivalence
→ final paper-level audit.

### Release gates
A question cannot enter the final mock pool if:
- answer is uncertain
- source cannot be verified
- it is too close to a PYQ
- it is merely an obscure fact without exam-value
- its Hindi/English meaning differs
- distractor has accidental ambiguity
- question violates the intended format distribution
- the same concept is overrepresented
- it duplicates an existing immutable Question ID.

## Important boundary
The evidence currently available is enough to learn **how coaching papers are constructed and where their structure can diverge from UPSC**, but not enough to make a complete provider-wide 2026 error audit. The distinct Vajiram & Ravi 2026 paper and the remaining 2026 mock files should be analyzed when they are available.
